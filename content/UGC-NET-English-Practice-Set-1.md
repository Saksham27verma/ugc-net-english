> The habit of sustained reading is not a natural endowment but an acquired discipline, and like every discipline it is sustained by a community rather than by solitary resolve. A reader who has nobody to talk to about what she reads will, sooner or later, read less. This is why the decline of reading in our time is not primarily a failure of individual will; it is a failure of shared attention. We have built institutions that reward the accumulation of information and punish the slowness in which understanding grows. The examination measures what can be recalled in an hour; the classroom, pressed for time, teaches what can be measured. Meanwhile the student learns the most durable lesson of all — that a book is an obstacle standing between herself and her marks. Nothing will change until teachers are permitted to be slow. A curriculum that has room for silence, for re-reading, and for the honest admission that a text has not yet been understood is not a luxury added to education; it is the condition under which education becomes possible at all.
